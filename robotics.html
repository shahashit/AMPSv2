The future of repair · the long-term horizon

Where repair is heading. And how you get there in steps.

Automation is reaching the service floor one task at a time. Robots already change tyres, plug in charging cables and repair bumpers, and AI is learning from real service work. You do not need to bet on it today: businesses that document their work now, step by step, are ready when these tools make sense for them. Beyond the factory

Physical AI is spreading to every skilled task.

AP describes physical AI as machines with AI and sensors that perceive, decide and act in real-world environments. Wherever work depends on perception, hand-eye coordination and judgement, physical AI needs real-world data to learn from. It is moving from factory floors into the field and emergency response. Automotive repair, maintenance and service is next.

PerceptionSeeing the taskRecognising parts, tools, damage and condition, as a skilled person does.
Hand-eye coordinationMoving with precisionGrip, angle, force and sequence, learned from real demonstrations.
JudgementDeciding what to doChoosing the next step, checking the result and handling the unexpected.

Where AMPS comes in: AMPS captures this kind of data in automotive service operations, during real repair, maintenance and service work, then anonymises and structures it so physical-AI models can learn skilled repair.

Where repair is heading

Three stages towards the robot-assisted service site.

This is the AMPS view of how automation will reach vehicle maintenance. It is our expectation, not a promise or a date. Each stage builds on data from the one before.

Stage 1 · Now Assisted and narrow automation Robots take single, well-defined tasks; AI supports diagnosis. Skilled technicians do the repair.
→Tyre and wheel robots→Charging robots→AI-supported diagnostics
Stage 2 · Next Robots learn from recorded repairs Models trained on video and motion data of real repairs begin to assist with repetitive service tasks.
→Learning from demonstration→Service and fast-fit tasks first→Technicians supervise
Stage 3 · Future The robot-assisted service floor Robots handle a growing share of standard maintenance; technicians lead diagnosis, quality and complex repair.
→Broader task coverage→Human-robot teams→Data-driven repair standards
Repair robotics today

Robots are already working on vehicles.

Physical AI already handles narrow, well-defined jobs on vehicles: wheels, charging, bumper repair and AI-supported diagnostics. Each one shows where broader repair automation can start.

Robots that remove, lift and fit wheels RoboTire systems at Discount Tire stores handle the wheel work while technicians operate them. The company says it cuts a roughly hour-long job to under 25 minutes. Source: Dallas Innovates ↗

How robots learn manual skills

They watch skilled hands, then practise.

Manual work is hard to program step by step. Increasingly, robots learn it from demonstration instead: video and motion data of a skilled person doing the task, turned into a model that predicts the right movement in each situation. The quality and variety of those demonstrations decide how well the robot performs.

01 MovementHow hands and tools move: grip, angle, force, sequence.
02 PerspectiveFirst-person video showing what the technician sees.
03 TimingHow long each step takes in real conditions.
04 ContextWhich vehicle, which fault, which decision, which check.

The missing ingredient

Factories record their work. Service sites don't, yet.

A car factory repeats the same steps on the same model thousands of times. A service bay sees a different vehicle, fault and condition in every bay. That variety is exactly what a repair robot must learn, and it is rarely recorded anywhere.

The gap Every vehicle is different Brands, models, ages and conditions vary in every bay. Robots must learn that variety.
What AMPS captures Repairs recorded across brands, repair areas and service sites.
The gap Know-how lives in people How an experienced technician approaches a job is rarely written down.
What AMPS captures First-person video of hand-eye coordination and key decisions.
The gap Privacy is non-negotiable Service footage contains people, plates and customer details.
What AMPS captures Data anonymised before processing, with clients informed upfront.
Which repair tasks come first

Not every job automates at the same pace.

Our view on how the main repair areas line up for automation. The more repetitive and standardised the task, the sooner robots can assist. The more judgement it needs, the longer people lead and AI advises.

Repair areaAutomation outlookLikely first stepData that helps most
Tyres & fast-fit Early Wheel removal, lifting and fitting Wheel handling, cycle times
EV & high-voltage Early (charging) Automated charging connection Safety routines, battery handling
Service & maintenance Emerging Repetitive service steps Task sequences, tool handling, timing
Body, paint & smart repair Research Defined repairs such as bumpers Fine-motor technique, surface prep
Diagnostics & ADAS AI-assisted AI suggests, technician decides Fault-tracing paths, decision points
Truck, bus & heavy Longer term Assisted handling of heavy parts Heavy-part handling, multi-person flows
Outlook reflects AMPS's assessment, not a prediction with fixed timelines.
People and robots

Robots take the strain. Technicians keep the judgement.

In the deployments that exist today, robots take on the heavy, repetitive part of a job while technicians operate them and handle the rest. At Discount Tire stores, for example, RoboTire systems remove, lift and fit wheels while technicians operate them. We expect the future of vehicle service to work the same way: robots for repetition and load, people for diagnosis, quality and the unexpected.

RobotsRepetitionThe same movement, many times a day.
RobotsPhysical loadLifting wheels, holding heavy parts.
TechniciansDiagnosisFinding the real cause of a fault.
TechniciansQuality & exceptionsChecking the result and handling the unexpected.
